## The work

SA Studio is a joint website study with Sanjay Kumar Gautam. The core design question was how a studio could explain itself through one continuous experience. We gave each of eight sections a role, then worked on the handoffs: scene continuity, copy arriving at the right frame, shared cursor behavior and controls that remain understandable as the scene changes. The responsive version preserves that sequence at smaller widths. The current evidence is a local work-in-progress implementation with dated Chromium captures and recorded checks. The study is useful because it reveals both authored visual direction and the engineering needed to carry that direction across a whole site.

## Problem

A studio website can become a stack of unrelated sections. We explored a continuous sequence in which each section has a clear job and hands the visitor into the next without losing orientation.

## Decisions

- Connect eight sections through shared scene handoffs and presented-frame copy, instead of treating each section as an isolated landing page.
- Use a shared cursor and section-aware controls to keep interaction coherent across the journey.
- Translate the composition to smaller viewports and preserve accessible alternative paths.

## Evidence and current scope

The September 2026 local polish candidate includes eight connected sections and recorded browser/build validation. Desktop and mobile-width Chromium screenshots show the actual local composition. The source repository is private, and these captures do not establish a public deployment or physical Safari/iPhone acceptance.

## Tradeoffs

The continuous experience makes sequencing and shared control handovers part of the engineering problem. Keep the joint authorship visible, label the current study WIP, and use the existing public portfolio as general context until a shared public study is approved.
